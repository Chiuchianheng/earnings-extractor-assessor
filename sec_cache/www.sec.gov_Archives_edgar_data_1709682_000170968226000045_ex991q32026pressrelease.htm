EXHIBIT 99.1

Custom Truck One Source, Inc. Reports Record Third Quarter 2026 Results and Increases Full Year 2026 Adjusted EBITDA and SER Revenue Guidance

Company Continues to Benefit from Grid Modernization, Electrification, Data Center, and Infrastructure Growth Through Disciplined Execution Focused on Profitable Growth and Deleveraging
KANSAS CITY, Mo. October 26, 2026 – (BUSINESS WIRE) – Custom Truck One Source, Inc. (NYSE: CTOS), a leading provider of specialty equipment to the electric utility, telecom, rail, forestry, waste management and other infrastructure-related end markets, today reported financial results for the three and nine months ended September 30, 2026.
CTOS Third-Quarter Highlights
•Revenue of $495.2 million, an increase of $13.2 million, or 2.7%, compared to the third quarter of 2025, with rental revenue up 20.1%; more than $20.0 million of third-party STEM deliveries shifted into the fourth quarter
•Record average OEC on rent of $1.39 billion, up $132.2 million, or 10.5%, compared to the third quarter of 2025, with fleet utilization of 81.5% and OEC on rent yield of 39.4%
•Gross profit of $113.3 million, an increase of $12.5 million, or 12.4%, compared to the third quarter of 2025
•Adjusted Gross Profit of $170.2 million, an increase of $14.7 million, or 9.4%, compared to the third quarter of 2025
•Net income of $2.3 million, an improvement of $8.1 million, compared to the third quarter of 2025
•Adjusted EBITDA of $110.9 million, an increase of $15.0 million, or 15.6%, compared to the third quarter of 2025; Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded approximately 250 basis points to 22.4%
•SER segment Adjusted EBITDA of $121.9 million, an increase of 22.5% compared to the third quarter of 2025, with segment Adjusted EBITDA margin expanding to 54.6% from 52.4%
•Reduced net leverage ratio to 3.70x, compared to 4.31x at year-end 2025 and 4.53x at the end of the third quarter 2025, even as ending fleet OEC grew $143.0 million year-over-year to a record $1.76 billion
•Raising 2026 SER segment revenue guidance to $870 million to $890 million, from $850 million to $875 million, on continued strength in transmission and distribution (“T&D”) end markets
•Raising CTOS Adjusted EBITDA1 guidance range to $447.5 million to $460 million, from $437.5 million to $455 million, the third increase this year and representing 17% to 20% growth over 2025
•Raising 2026 net rental fleet investment to $210 million to $230 million to capture visible, high-return transmission rental demand; 2026 levered free cashflow is now expected to be slightly positive, with the earnings benefit of the incremental fleet weighted to 2027

“In the third quarter, record demand across our core T&D markets drove 16% year-over-year Adjusted EBITDA growth and 24% growth year to date, and we are raising our full-year Adjusted EBITDA outlook for the third time this year. Our SER segment continued to benefit from strong utility end-market demand, with our rental fleet generating a record quarterly average OEC on rent of nearly $1.4 billion and average utilization of 81.5%. We also ended the quarter with record total OEC of $1.76 billion, providing strong visibility into continued SER growth through the fourth quarter and into 2027,” said Ryan McMonagle, Chief Executive Officer of CTOS. “In STEM, orders grew 22% year-over-year and backlog reached $342 million, and the third-party deliveries that shifted out of the quarter are scheduled to ship in the fourth quarter. Our earnings growth also drove a further meaningful reduction in net leverage, to 3.70x. As we look to the remainder of 2026 and beyond, CTOS is well positioned to participate in powerful secular trends, including utility grid upgrades and transmission expansion, electrification, data center-driven load growth, and infrastructure spending. We remain disciplined in executing our strategy, with a focus on profitable growth, strategic capital deployment, free cash flow generation, and continued deleveraging. Increasing our investment in the rental fleet reflects that discipline as we put capital behind visible, long-duration transmission demand at attractive returns, with the larger earnings benefit arriving in 2027.”

Summary Actual Financial Results by Segment
Beginning January 1, 2026, CTOS is reporting our results under two reportable segments: (1) Specialty Equipment Rentals (“SER”) and (2) Specialty Truck Equipment and Manufacturing (“STEM”). The new SER segment consists of our historical Equipment Rental Solutions (“ERS”) segment (except for certain used sales to be accounted for by STEM) and a portion of our historical Aftermarket Parts and Services (“APS”) segment, and the new STEM segment consists of our historical Truck and Equipment Sales (“TES”) segment, certain used sales that previously were accounted for by ERS and a portion of our historical APS segment. We are also reflecting intercompany activity between the two segments, which is ultimately eliminated in consolidation. This new segment reporting reflects how CTOS’s business is managed and how resources are allocated in 2026 and utilizes Adjusted EBITDA as the segments’ profit measure. Segment Adjusted EBITDA is defined as segment operating income or loss before depreciation and amortization, further excluding the effects of purchase accounting adjustments and the impact of sales-type lease accounting for certain leases containing rental purchase options (or “RPOs”).
Management believes this new presentation better reflects the positioning of CTOS’s strategies and operations portfolio and better reflects key economic drivers, capital intensity, and margin profiles of the respective new segments, as well as aligns our external reporting with how management allocates capital and evaluates performance. Prior period amounts have been recast to reflect the change to two reportable segments.

Management Commentary
The increase of 20.1% in rental revenue in the third quarter of 2026 compared to the third quarter of 2025 was the result of improved average fleet utilization (which increased to 81.5% compared to 79.3%) driven by increased rental volume, with average OEC on rent increasing by 10.5% year-over-year and OEC on rent yield improving to 39.4%. Compared to the third quarter of 2025, SER rental equipment sales increased 14.1% in the third quarter of 2026 due to an increase in buyout activity of rental contracts with purchase options. SER adjusted EBITDA in the third quarter of 2026 increased 22.5% compared to the third quarter of 2025. SER segment Adjusted EBITDA margin expanded to 54.6% from 52.4% in the prior-year period, and adjusted rental gross margin reached 78.7%.
Total revenue in our STEM segment increased 8% in the third quarter of 2026 compared to the third quarter of 2025 driven by higher intercompany sales to SER to support increased rental activity. This increase was partially offset by a 5.5% decline in new equipment sales driven by lower third-party unit sales volume. Third-party sales were impacted by our strategic decision to prioritize our production for additional investment in the rental fleet to take advantage of current and anticipated rental demand. In addition, unit sales volume was adversely impacted by both a vendor recall, as well as unusual damage to some finished vehicle inventory caused by a hailstorm to our Kansas City location. While these events resulted in reduced sales of more than $20.0 million, we believe they represent only temporary timing issues and that the sales delayed by the vendor recall and hailstorm will be completed in the fourth quarter. Excluding these shifted deliveries, third-party STEM revenue would have exceeded the third quarter of 2025. STEM segment Adjusted EBITDA was $36.6 million in the third quarter of 2026; 2025 segment results reflect intersegment sales at no margin and are not directly comparable. Our STEM backlog increased 22% compared to the third quarter of 2025, and, at approximately 3.8 months of LTM third-party new sales, sits modestly below our targeted range of four to six months.


The increase in net income in the third quarter of 2026, compared to a loss in the third quarter of 2025, was primarily driven by higher operating income resulting from growth in rental revenue due to higher average OEC on rent, as well as lower interest expense.
Adjusted EBITDA for the third quarter of 2026 was $110.9 million, a 15.6% increase compared to the third quarter of 2025, which was largely driven by increased gross profit and lower floor plan interest expense resulting from lower inventory levels.
As of September 30, 2026, cash and cash equivalents were $14.6 million, total debt outstanding was $1,666.0 million, net debt was $1,651.5 million and our net leverage ratio was 3.70x. Availability under the senior secured credit facility was $234.3 million as of September 30, 2026, and based on our borrowing base, we have an additional $288.0 million of suppressed availability that we could access by upsizing our existing facility.

2026 Outlook
We are increasing our full year 2026 SER segment revenue and Adjusted EBITDA1, 4 guidance and raising our net rental fleet investment forecast to reflect record rental performance and sustained demand across our utility T&D end markets.
Consolidated CTOS:
•Revenue is expected to increase 8% to 13% year-over-year, with Adjusted EBITDA1, 4 expected to increase 17% to 20%.
•Net rental fleet investment (purchases less proceeds) for 2026 is expected to be approximately $210 million to $230 million, up from prior guidance of $170 million to $200 million to capture visible, high-return rental demand, while still reflecting a reduction from over $250 million in 2025.
•Inventory ended the third quarter at $1.016 billion, down $26 million from the second quarter. Year-end 2026 inventory months on hand is expected to be approximately flat with year-end 2025, as we prepare for the expected growth in 2027 and the build-up of chassis inventory under our pre-buy program ahead of 2027 emissions regulations.
•Levered free cash flow2, 4 for 2026 is now expected to be slightly positive, compared to prior guidance of more than $50 million, reflecting the increase in net rental fleet investment and the working capital associated with growth. This is a deliberate reallocation of 2026 cash flow toward rental fleet growth with visible, high returns; our underlying earnings outlook has strengthened, as reflected in the higher Adjusted EBITDA guidance. Net leverage ratio3, 4 was 3.70x at September 30, 2026, and is expected to be meaningfully below four times at the end of fiscal 2026; the longer-term target remains achieving a net leverage ratio3, 4 below three times in 2027.
Specialty Equipment Rentals (SER):
•The rental business delivered record results in the quarter, with average OEC on rent of approximately $1.4 billion, average fleet utilization of 81.5% and OEC on rent yield of 39.4% all ahead of expectations. OEC on rent and utilization have continued to grow in the fourth quarter, surpassing $1.5 billion and 84%, respectively, in October.
•Demand for equipment serving the utility transmission and distribution market remains at record levels, led by unprecedented transmission demand, with OEC on rent up more than $132 million year-over-year in the third quarter; further penetration of the vocational rental market, where OEC on rent increased approximately $45 million year-over-year, is expected to provide incremental growth.
•In response to this demand, we are increasing 2026 gross rental fleet investment to approximately $400 million to $430 million, with the incremental investment concentrated in long-term transmission rentals and high-return specialty products. Given delivery timing, the revenue and earnings contribution from these additions will be modest in 2026, with the larger benefit expected in 2027. Average fleet age remained approximately three years at the end of the third quarter, and ending OEC is expected to increase by a mid-to-high single digit percentage in 2026.
Specialty Truck Equipment & Manufacturing (STEM):
•Third-party new sales revenue is expected to increase 3% to 10% in 2026 compared to 2025, with fourth quarter third-party new sales expected to increase at a mid-to-high-teens rate year-over-year as deliveries delayed in the third quarter are completed, supported by continued customer demand, improving product availability and lead times and relationships with key customers, chassis suppliers and attachment suppliers.
•Total STEM revenue is expected to be down marginally to up 3% year-over-year, with results now expected toward the upper end of the range as higher intersegment sales to support rental fleet growth supplement third-party growth.
•Sales order backlog ended the third quarter at $342 million, up 22% year-over-year and 6% sequentially, or approximately 3.8 months of LTM third-party new sales, modestly below the targeted range of four to six months. More than $20 million of third-party deliveries shifted out of the third quarter as a result of a hailstorm at our Kansas City facility and a vendor warranty recall; these units remain in backlog and are expected to be delivered in the fourth quarter. Third quarter orders increased 22% year-over-year, led by the utility, vacuum, refuse, rail and trailer markets, supporting revenue conversion in the fourth quarter and into 2027.



“Third quarter results reflect what we believe are the early stages of what could be a once-in-a-generation transmission demand super-cycle in our utility end markets. Average OEC on rent and total fleet OEC both set new records, utilization and yield remained at or near record levels, and OEC on rent has continued to climb, surpassing $1.5 billion in October. That visibility is why we are raising our net rental fleet investment outlook to $210 million to $230 million and increasing our full-year SER revenue and Adjusted EBITDA1, 4 guidance,” said Chris Eperjesy, Chief Financial Officer of CTOS. “In STEM, we prioritized production capacity for our own rental fleet, and more than $20 million of third-party deliveries shifted out of the quarter as a result of a hailstorm and a vendor recall. Those units remain in backlog and are expected to ship in the fourth quarter, and with orders up 22% year-over-year, we expect fourth quarter consolidated revenue and Adjusted EBITDA1, 4 to be up both year-over-year and sequentially. The incremental fleet investment moderates our 2026 levered free cash flow2, 4 expectations, but it is being deployed against visible, longer-term, high-return demand whose earnings contribution will largely arrive in 2027. We ended the quarter with net leverage3, 4 of 3.70x, down more than 0.8x year-over-year even as we grew the fleet by more than $140 million, and we remain on track to be meaningfully below four times at year-end and to reach our target of below three times in 2027. With LTM Adjusted EBITDA1, 4 now above $446 million, we currently expect another year of double-digit Adjusted EBITDA1, 4 growth in 2027.”

ABOUT CUSTOM TRUCK ONE SOURCE
Custom Truck One Source is one of the largest providers of specialty equipment, parts, tools, accessories and services to the electric utility transmission and distribution, and infrastructure end markets in North America. The Company rents and sells its specialized equipment to a diverse customer base for the maintenance, repair, upgrade and installation of critical infrastructure assets, including electric lines, telecommunications networks and rail systems. The Company's coast-to-coast rental fleet of more than 10,850 units includes aerial devices, boom trucks, cranes, digger derricks, vacuum trucks, pressure drills, stringing gear, hi-rail equipment, water trucks, dump trucks, repair parts, tools and accessories. NON-GAAP FINANCIAL AND PERFORMANCE MEASURES
In our press release and schedules, and on the related conference call, we report certain financial measures that are not required by, or presented in accordance with, United States generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”). We utilize these financial measures to manage our business on a day-to-day basis and some of these measures are commonly used in our industry to evaluate performance by excluding items considered to be non-recurring. We believe these non-GAAP measures provide investors expanded insight to assess performance, in addition to the standard GAAP-based financial measures. The press release schedules reconcile the most directly comparable GAAP measure to each non-GAAP measure that we refer to. Although management evaluates and presents these non-GAAP measures for the reasons described herein, please be aware that these non-GAAP measures have limitations and should not be considered in isolation or as a substitute for revenue, operating income/loss, net income/loss, earnings/loss per share or any other comparable measure prescribed by GAAP. In addition, we may calculate and/or present these non-GAAP financial measures differently than measures with the same or similar names that other companies report, and as a result, the non-GAAP measures we report may not be comparable to those reported by others.
Adjusted EBITDA. Adjusted EBITDA is a non-GAAP performance measure that we use to monitor our results of operations, to measure performance against debt covenants and performance relative to competitors. We believe Adjusted EBITDA is a useful performance measure because it allows for an effective evaluation of operating performance, without regard to financing methods or capital structures. We exclude the items identified in the reconciliations of net income (loss) to Adjusted EBITDA because these amounts are either non-recurring or can vary substantially within the industry depending upon accounting methods and book values of assets, including the method by which the assets were acquired, and capital structures. Adjusted EBITDA should not be considered as an alternative to, or more meaningful than, net income (loss) determined in accordance with GAAP. Certain items excluded from Adjusted EBITDA are significant components in understanding and assessing a company’s financial performance, such as a company’s cost of capital and tax structure, as well as the historical costs of depreciable assets, none of which are reflected in Adjusted EBITDA. Our presentation of Adjusted EBITDA should not be construed as an indication that results will be unaffected by the items excluded from Adjusted EBITDA. Our computation of Adjusted EBITDA may not be identical to other similarly titled measures of other companies.
We define Adjusted EBITDA as net income or loss before interest expense (excluding interest on floorplan financing), income taxes, depreciation and amortization, share-based compensation, and other items that we do not view as indicative of ongoing performance. Our Adjusted EBITDA includes an adjustment to exclude the effects of purchase accounting adjustments when calculating the cost of inventory and used equipment sold. When inventory or equipment is purchased in connection with a business combination, the assets are revalued to their current fair values for accounting purposes. The consideration transferred (i.e., the purchase price) in a business combination is allocated to the fair values of the assets as of the acquisition date, with amortization or depreciation recorded thereafter following applicable accounting policies; however, this may not be indicative of the actual cost to acquire inventory or new equipment that is added to product inventory or the rental fleets apart from a business acquisition. We also include an adjustment to remove the impact of accounting for certain of our rental contracts with customers containing a rental purchase option that are accounted for under GAAP as a sales-type lease. We include this adjustment because we believe continuing to reflect the transactions as an operating lease better reflects the economics of the transactions given our large portfolio of rental contracts. These, and other, adjustments to GAAP net income or loss that are applied to derive Adjusted EBITDA are specified by our senior secured credit agreement and the indenture of our senior secured notes.
Adjusted Gross Profit. We present total gross profit excluding rental equipment depreciation (“Adjusted Gross Profit”) as a non-GAAP financial performance measure. This measure differs from the GAAP definition of gross profit, as we do not include the impact of depreciation expense, which represents non-cash expense. We use this measure to evaluate operating margins and the effectiveness of the cost of our rental fleet.
Net Debt. We present the non-GAAP financial measure “Net Debt,” which is total debt (the most comparable GAAP measure, calculated as current and long-term debt, excluding deferred financing fees, plus current and long-term finance lease obligations) minus cash and cash equivalents. We believe this non-GAAP measure is useful to investors to evaluate our financial position.
Net Leverage Ratio. Net leverage ratio is a non-GAAP performance measure used by management and we believe it provides useful information to investors because it is an important measure to evaluate our debt levels and progress toward leverage targets, which is consistent with the manner our lenders and management use this measure. We define net leverage ratio as net debt divided by Adjusted EBITDA for the previous twelve-month period (“last twelve months,” or “LTM”).
